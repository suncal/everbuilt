Our clients pay $0/month for hosting. Forever. Here's the architecture, since "that's impossible" is the most common reply:

→ The site is static files — no server, no database, no CMS to patch
→ Served from a global edge network (the same class of infrastructure the biggest tech companies use) whose free tier includes unlimited bandwidth
→ Content the client updates themselves: photos through an admin panel, events through a spreadsheet that syncs to the site in minutes
→ Forms wired straight to email with a redundant capture safety net

Proof it holds at scale: we launched a national nonprofit tour's site this way. 2,100+ photos. 78GB of media processed. Zero minutes of downtime during the domain cutover. Hosting bill since launch day: $0.00.

The uncomfortable part for my industry: most "maintenance" contracts exist because the architecture was chosen to need maintenance.

If your website costs you every month, the question isn't "is my vendor overcharging" — it's "why does this architecture have a meter on it at all?"
